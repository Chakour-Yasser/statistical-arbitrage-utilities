# Survivorship bias, quantification and direction

Generated by `scripts/build_survivorship_en.py`. Source: `reports/identity_validation.csv`.
French version: `reports/survivorship.md`.

## 1. What point-in-time fixes

Index membership is read from the Wikipedia page **as it existed** on each selection
date (25 semi-annual snapshots, 2-day median lag). The screener therefore never sees a
name before it entered the index, nor after it left.

The clearest case: **PCG (PG&E)** is out of the index from 2019-07 to 2022-07 , 
Chapter 11 after the Camp Fire, removal, then readmission. A universe built on
**current** constituents would include PCG and trade it through the bankruptcy,
knowing it came out alive. Point-in-time excludes it automatically.

## 2. What remains uncorrected

Raw union: **38 tickers**. Tradable universe: **33**.
Excluded: **5** (GAS, NU, POM, TE, TEG).

| Ticker | Member snapshots | Status | Reason |

| `GAS` | 6 | NO_DATA | AGL Resources, acquired by Southern Company (2016). No data served: outright loss. |
| `NU` | 3 | recycled | Northeast Utilities, **renamed Eversource (ES)** in 2015. Ticker reused since 2021 by Nu Holdings (Brazilian neobank). Only an apparent loss: the ES series goes back to 2014 and carries the history. |
| `POM` | 5 | recycled | Pepco Holdings, acquired by Exelon (2016). Ticker recycled in 2025. |
| `TE` | 6 | recycled | TECO Energy, acquired by Emera (2016). Ticker recycled in 2020. |
| `TEG` | 4 | recycled | Integrys Energy, acquired by WEC (2015). Ticker recycled in late 2015. |

All of these exits are **index removals by acquisition** (no bankruptcy).

## 3. Magnitude

- Membership slots (snapshot x ticker): 732 raw, 708 kept → **3.3 % lost**.
- Losses are concentrated in 2014-2016; from 2019-07 onward they are zero.

| Snapshot | Sector (raw) | Tradable | Lost | Testable pairs |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2014-01 | 31 | 26 | 5 | 465 → 325 |
| 2014-07 | 30 | 25 | 5 | 435 → 300 |
| 2015-01 | 30 | 25 | 5 | 435 → 300 |
| 2015-07 | 30 | 26 | 4 | 435 → 325 |
| 2016-01 | 29 | 26 | 3 | 406 → 325 |
| 2016-07 | 29 | 27 | 2 | 406 → 351 |

**Order of magnitude.** Losses are entirely concentrated in the first six snapshots
(2014-01 to 2016-07) and **zero from 2017-01**. At the worst snapshot (2014-01), 5 names
are missing out of 31, 16 % of the universe, i.e. 1-(26/31)^2 ≈ **30 % of potential
pairs** untestable. From 2017 the residual survivorship on composition is nil.

Practical consequence for the walk-forward: the first fold (selection 2014-2016) is the
most exposed. An honest option is to report its result separately rather than silently
aggregating it with the others.

## 4. Direction of the bias

The five lost names all left the index **by acquisition**. A takeover is precisely the
event that breaks a cointegration relation most brutally: the target's price jumps to
the offer price and then freezes, permanently decorrelated from its sector. A short
position on the target at announcement takes an instant, non-mean-reverting loss.

**Consequence: their exclusion Overstates the strategy's performance.** The bias runs
in the favourable direction, which is the most dangerous case, it never announces
itself.

## 5. Limits of the reconstruction itself

- Wikipedia is edited with a lag of a few days (median 2 d, max 19 d here). No effect at
  an annual re-selection cadence.
- **EVRG** (Evergy, formed in 2018 from the Westar/Great Plains merger) has vendor
  history back to 2014: those are the predecessor's prices. Legitimate, but worth noting.
- The identity check rests on the overlap between quotation and membership. It would
  less reliably catch a ticker recycling occurring *during* the membership window, a
  case not observed here.
